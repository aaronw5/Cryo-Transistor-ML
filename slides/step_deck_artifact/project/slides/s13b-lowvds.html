pMOS at low |Vds|: fitted cards vs measured

  • |Vds| 0.01 V, |Vgs| 1.81-1.85 V: ln(I_card / I_meas) pMOS +0.21 to +1.21 (7p), +0.15 to +1.20 (45p), card above measured on 10 of 10 (7p) and 10 of 10 (45p); nMOS -0.26 to +0.20 / -0.25 to +0.02
  • Id-Vd at |Vgs| 1.85 V, |Vds| = 0.2 VDD: card below measured on 10 of 10 pMOS (7p) and 10 of 10 (45p); conductance ratio G(0.15 VDD) / G(0.03 VDD), G = |Id| / |Vds|: measured 0.98-1.34 (> 1 on 7 of 10), 7p card 0.89-0.97, 45p card 0.89-0.98 (> 1 on 0 and 0 of 10)
  • Same bias in two measured sweeps (|Vgs| 1.85 V, |Vds| 0.01 V): Id-Vd / Id-Vg current pMOS 0.28-1.13 (< 1 on 9 of 10), nMOS 1.15-1.31; one card value cannot match both sweeps there
On all ten pMOS devices both fitted cards give more current than measured in the Id-Vg sweep at a drain voltage of 0.01 V; on nMOS the difference is small and of both signs. Along the pMOS Id-Vd curve at the highest gate voltage, the cards are below the measurement at 0.1 to 0.3 of the supply voltage. Together this means the measured pMOS channel conductance rises with drain voltage at small drain voltage, while the conductance of both fitted cards falls: with the fitted 7 or 45 parameters the card does not produce this shape. Going from 7 to 45 parameters lowers the excess at 0.01 V but does not remove it. Part of the difference is in the data: at the same bias point the measured Id-Vd and Id-Vg sweeps of the pMOS differ by up to a factor 3.6, so no single card value matches both.
